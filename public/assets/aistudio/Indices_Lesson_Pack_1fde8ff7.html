Scenario: Rewind the Views

A video has exactly 1000 views at noon, and its views double every hour. Students are asked how many views it had at 3 pm, then how many it had at 11 am and at 9 am. Going forward is easy. Going backward forces the question of what a power means when the index is zero or negative.

Count in thousands of views and call noon hour 0. Hour n has 2n thousand views. The table below stays on screen all lesson and every rule is discovered from it.

Hour slider

Values in thousands of views. Tap a cell to jump to that hour.

Run sheet

  1. 5 min

    Predict the views

    Show the scenario. Students write predictions for 3 pm, 11 am and 9 am on mini whiteboards. Collect them without judging.

    Listen for 500 at 11 am. That is the first negative index in the room.

  2. 10 min

    Build the table

    Students fill the hour table from −3 to 5 as powers of 2. Moving right multiplies by 2 and moving left divides by 2. Ask what sits at hour 0, then why 2−1 must be a half.

    Use the slider on the projector to confirm. Name the two results: a0 = 1 and a−n = 1/an.

  3. 10 min

    Discover the laws

    Pose three questions and let pairs answer from the table. After 3 hours and then 2 more hours, how many times larger is the video? After 5 hours forward and 2 back? How many times more views at hour 5 than at hour 2?

    Link each to 23 × 22, 25 × 2−2 and 25 ÷ 22. Check each with ordinary numbers (8 × 4 = 32). Students state the pattern in their own words before you show the rules.

  4. 5 min

    Change the base

    Switch the slider to ×10 and ×3. The rules still hold. Cold-call examples on whiteboards, such as 104 ÷ 106 and 3−2 × 35.

  5. 15 min

    Practise

    Pairs work through Worksheet A. Students use Check my answers after each part and fix the red boxes. Circulate and listen for the mistakes listed below.

  6. 5 min

    Challenge and exit ticket

    Early finishers start Worksheet B. Everyone answers the exit ticket before leaving.

Key ideas

a0 = 1

Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1. It is the starting point of the table.

a−n = 1 ÷ an

A negative index gives a reciprocal. The answer is a positive fraction, not a negative number.

am × an = am+n

Same base: keep the base and add the indices.

am ÷ an = am−n

Same base: keep the base and subtract the indices, in that order.

Success criteria

  • I can evaluate powers with positive, zero and negative indices, and write negative-index answers as fractions.
  • I can write products and quotients of powers with the same base as a single power.
  • I can find a missing index.

Common mistakes

  • 2−3 = −8 or −6. Walk left along the table. Each step halves the value, so it never goes below zero.
  • 50 = 0 or 5. Point to the hour 0 cell. Nothing has been multiplied, so the value is 1.
  • 23 × 24 = 47. The base does not change. 8 × 16 = 128 = 27, but 47 = 16 384.
  • 32 × 34 = 38. Students multiply the indices. Check with 9 × 81 = 729 = 36.
  • 22 ÷ 25 = 23. Subtracting the wrong way round. A small number divided by a large one is below 1, so the index must be negative.

Exit ticket

1. Evaluate 60.

2. Write 54 ÷ 56 as a single power, then as a fraction.

3. Write 2−3 × 25 as a single power, then evaluate it.

Answers: 1 · 5−2 = 1/25 · 22 = 4

Differentiation

  • Support: keep to bases 2 and 10 and let students use the table for every question.
  • Stretch: Worksheet B, which moves to letters as indices and unknown indices.
